THE MISSION

The Word of Christ,
accessible to every believer.

Scripture was not given to scholars. It was given to the world. Jesus Christ — the Living Word — spoke in Aramaic, the language of common people. The Apostles wrote in Greek, the language of the known world. The earliest Church Fathers wrote for every believer, not only the learned. The Word of God was always meant to be within reach.

MILTHA exists to restore that reach. Every scripture in the language it was originally written. Every word tappable, explainable, traceable to its root. The Church Fathers — those who sat at the feet of the Apostles — speaking directly to you without a modern interpreter standing between. The historical record, the archaeology, the secular sources — all confirming what Scripture declares.

This is not a Bible app. It is a digital sanctuary — built on the earliest manuscripts, anchored in verified history, and dedicated to one truth: Christ is King, and His Word endures.

THE CHURCH FATHERS

The voices before the denominations

These men predate every split in Christianity. Their testimony is the earliest commentary that exists — and the only commentary MILTHA trusts.

IGNATIUS OF ANTIOCH
c. 35–108 AD · BY TRADITION A DISCIPLE OF JOHN THE APOSTLE · MARTYR
Wrote seven letters on his way to martyrdom in Rome
"There is one Physician who is possessed both of flesh and spirit; both made and not made; God existing in flesh; true life in death; both of Mary and of God...even Jesus Christ our Lord."
POLYCARP OF SMYRNA
c. 69–155 AD · DISCIPLE OF JOHN THE APOSTLE · MARTYR
Burned alive at the age of 86 for refusing to deny Christ
"Eighty-six years I have served Him, and He never did me any wrong. How then can I blaspheme my King and my Savior?"
JUSTIN MARTYR
c. 100–165 AD · FIRST GREAT CHRISTIAN APOLOGIST · MARTYR
Presented the Christian faith directly to the Roman Emperor
"We who formerly used to murder one another do not only now refrain from making war...willingly die confessing Christ. And this is no mere verbal profession, but the fact really is so."
IRENAEUS OF LYON
c. 130–202 AD · STUDENT OF POLYCARP · BISHOP OF LYON
Heard Polycarp speak — who had learned from John the Apostle
"He became what we are that He might bring us to be even what He is Himself. The glory of God is a living man; and the life of man consists in beholding God."
