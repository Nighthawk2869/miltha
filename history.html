The oldest surviving copies of Hebrew Scripture. Discovered 1947. Identical to modern Bibles — proving no corruption over 2,000 years.

Ministry of Jesus · Galilee and Judea
Three years of documented public ministry confirmed by Jewish, Roman, and Christian sources. Eyewitnesses alive and writing within the same generation.

THE PILATE STONE
DISCOVERED: 1961 · CAESAREA MARITIMA
A limestone block bearing a Latin inscription: "Pontius Pilate, Prefect of Judea." Found during theater excavations. Now in the Israel Museum, Jerusalem.
John 19:1 — "Then Pilate took Jesus and flogged him." The man who condemned Jesus is confirmed by Rome's own inscription. The argument against His historical existence is permanently closed.
SOURCE: ISRAEL ANTIQUITIES AUTHORITY · EXCAVATED BY ANTONIO FROVA · 1961
🏛️
THE POOL OF SILOAM
EXCAVATED: 2004 · CITY OF DAVID, JERUSALEM
A large stepped pool from the Second Temple period, discovered during sewer repair work. Dated to the first century by pottery and coins. Five porticoes confirmed.
John 9:7 — "Go, wash in the Pool of Siloam." The steps where the blind man walked down are still there. The channel through which the healing water flowed has been identified.
SOURCE: ISRAEL ANTIQUITIES AUTHORITY · RONNY REICH AND ELI SHUKRON · 2004
⚱️
THE OSSUARY OF CAIAPHAS
DISCOVERED: 1990 · JERUSALEM
A bone box inscribed "Yehosef bar Qayafa" — Joseph son of Caiaphas. Found in a burial cave in Jerusalem. Bones of an elderly man inside. Now in the Israel Museum.
John 18:13 — "They led him first to Annas, the father-in-law of Caiaphas." The high priest who presided over Jesus's trial has a verified burial. He was a real man making a real decision.
SOURCE: ISRAEL ANTIQUITIES AUTHORITY · ZIAS AND REICH · 1992
📜
JOHN RYLANDS PAPYRUS P52
DATED: c. 125 AD · EGYPT
The oldest surviving New Testament fragment, containing John 18:31-33. Written in Greek. Found in Egypt. Now at the John Rylands Library, Manchester.
Proves John's Gospel circulated in Egypt within 30 years of composition — within living memory of the events it records. Any theory of late invention is permanently destroyed.
SOURCE: JOHN RYLANDS LIBRARY · UNIVERSITY OF MANCHESTER · C.H. ROBERTS · 1934
📜
THE DEAD SEA SCROLLS
DISCOVERED: 1947 · QUMRAN, ISRAEL
Over 900 manuscripts including the complete Isaiah scroll. Carbon dated 250 BC–68 AD. One thousand years older than any previously known Hebrew manuscripts.
The Isaiah scroll is virtually identical to the Masoretic text in modern Bibles — proving faithful preservation over a millennium. Isaiah 53, written 700 years before Christ, reads exactly as it does today.
SOURCE: ISRAEL ANTIQUITIES AUTHORITY · THE SHRINE OF THE BOOK, JERUSALEM
⛵
THE SEA OF GALILEE BOAT
DISCOVERED: 1986 · SEA OF GALILEE
A first-century wooden fishing boat recovered from the mud of the Sea of Galilee during a drought. Carbon dated 100 BC–70 AD. Now at the Yigal Allon Museum.
Mark 4:37-38 — "A great windstorm arose..." The exact type of boat Jesus and the disciples crossed the Sea of Galilee in is now visible in a museum on its shores.
SOURCE: YIGAL ALLON MUSEUM · KIBBUTZ GINOSAR · CARBON DATED BY OXFORD UNIVERSITY
TACITUS
ANNALS, BOOK XV, SECTION 44 · c. 116 AD · ROMAN SENATOR AND HISTORIAN
"Christus, from whom the name had its origin, suffered the extreme penalty during the reign of Tiberius at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus, and a most mischievous superstition, thus checked for the moment, again broke out not only in Judaea, the first source of the evil, but even in Rome..."
A Roman senator writing with explicit hostility toward Christianity independently confirms the execution of Christ under Pontius Pilate during the reign of Tiberius. Tacitus had no motive to support Christian claims — he calls Christianity a "mischievous superstition." Yet his account matches John 19 precisely.
SOURCE: TACITUS · ANNALS · BOOK XV · SECTION 44 · LOEB CLASSICAL LIBRARY
FLAVIUS JOSEPHUS
ANTIQUITIES OF THE JEWS, BOOK XX · c. 93 AD · FIRST-CENTURY JEWISH HISTORIAN
"...the brother of Jesus who was called Christ, whose name was James, and some others...he brought them before them, and accused them, and delivered them to be stoned."
A first-century Jewish historian writing for a Roman audience with no motive to support Christian claims. His casual reference to "Jesus who was called Christ" demonstrates that Jesus's existence was uncontested historical fact in the first century — requiring no argument or defense.
SOURCE: JOSEPHUS · JEWISH ANTIQUITIES · BOOK XX · LOEB CLASSICAL LIBRARY
PLINY THE YOUNGER
LETTERS, BOOK X, LETTER 96 · c. 112 AD · ROMAN GOVERNOR OF BITHYNIA-PONTUS
"They were in the habit of meeting on a certain fixed day before it was light and singing an anthem to Christ as to a god, and binding themselves by a solemn oath, not to any wicked deeds, but never to commit any fraud, theft or adultery, never to falsify their word..."
A Roman governor writing to Emperor Trajan for legal guidance confirms that within 80 years of the crucifixion, communities were worshipping Jesus as divine across the Roman Empire. He describes practices matching exactly what the New Testament records.
SOURCE: PLINY THE YOUNGER · LETTERS · BOOK X · LETTER 96 · LOEB CLASSICAL LIBRARY
THALLUS
HISTORY · c. 52 AD · SAMARITAN HISTORIAN (cited by Julius Africanus, c. 221 AD)
Thallus attempts to explain the darkness at the crucifixion as a solar eclipse — the earliest known non-Christian reference to the death of Jesus, written within 20 years of the event.
Writing only 20 years after the crucifixion, Thallus acknowledges the unusual darkness and tries to explain it naturally — confirming that the event was widely known and required explanation even among non-believers. Julius Africanus notes a solar eclipse during Passover (a full moon) is astronomically impossible, meaning the darkness was extraordinary.
SOURCE: THALLUS · FRAGMENT 18 · CITED IN JULIUS AFRICANUS · CHRONOGRAPHY · c. 221 AD
