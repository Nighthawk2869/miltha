OLD TESTAMENT ERA
c. 1000 BC
Psalms of David Written
Psalm 22, Psalm 110, and other Messianic Psalms composed — directly quoted by Jesus as prophecies fulfilled in Christ.
SOURCE: HEBREW MASORETIC TEXT · DEAD SEA SCROLLS CONFIRM STABILITY
c. 700 BC
Isaiah Writes the Suffering Servant
Isaiah 53 — the most detailed Messianic prophecy. The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (c. 125 BC) shows the chapter was written and copied long before Jesus's birth.
SOURCE: GREAT ISAIAH SCROLL (1QIsaᵃ) · DEAD SEA SCROLLS · ISRAEL MUSEUM · c. 125 BC
c. 250 BC – 68 AD
Dead Sea Scrolls Copied · Qumran
Among the oldest surviving copies of Hebrew Scripture. Discovered from 1947. Their biblical texts are remarkably close to the Masoretic text of modern Bibles — most differences are spelling and small wording variants.
SOURCE: ISRAEL ANTIQUITIES AUTHORITY · CARBON DATED · THE SHRINE OF THE BOOK, JERUSALEM
THE LIFE OF CHRIST
c. 6–4 BC — BIRTH
Birth of Jesus · Bethlehem, Judea
Before the death of Herod the Great (4 BC), whose reign Josephus records (Matthew 2:1; Luke 1:5). No Roman census record naming Jesus survives. Fulfillment of Micah 5:2 — written some 700 years earlier.
SOURCE: MATTHEW 2 · LUKE 2:1-7 · JOSEPHUS · ANTIQUITIES XVII (DEATH OF HEROD)
c. 27–30 AD — MINISTRY
Ministry of Jesus · Galilee and Judea
About three years of public ministry, by the count of Passovers in John's Gospel. Josephus and Tacitus independently attest to Jesus and his execution. The Gospels were written within the lifetime of eyewitnesses.
SOURCE: JOSEPHUS ANTIQUITIES XVIII · TACITUS ANNALS XV · THE FOUR GOSPELS
c. 30–33 AD — CRUCIFIXION & RESURRECTION
Death and Resurrection · Jerusalem
Executed under Pontius Pilate — recorded by Tacitus (Annals XV.44) and Josephus. The Pilate Stone (found 1961) confirms Pilate as prefect of Judea.
SOURCE: TACITUS ANNALS XV.44 · THE PILATE STONE · ISRAEL MUSEUM
THE EARLY CHURCH
c. 49–67 AD
Paul's Letters Written
The earliest New Testament documents — within 20 years of the crucifixion. 1 Corinthians 15 records a creed scholars date within 3–5 years of the event.
SOURCE: 1 CORINTHIANS 15:3-8 · EARLIEST CREED c. 33–36 AD
70 AD
Destruction of the Temple · Jerusalem
Roman General Titus destroys the Second Temple, fulfilling Jesus's prophecy in Matthew 24:1-2. The Western Wall, part of the retaining wall of the Temple Mount, still stands.
SOURCE: JOSEPHUS · JEWISH WAR VI · THE ARCH OF TITUS, ROME
c. 85–95 AD
Gospel of John Written · Ephesus
The Rylands Papyrus P52 — among the oldest surviving New Testament fragments — contains part of John 18. Dated by its handwriting to the first half of the 2nd century AD (some scholars date it later).
SOURCE: JOHN RYLANDS PAPYRUS P52 · JOHN RYLANDS LIBRARY, MANCHESTER
c. 2ND–5TH CENTURY AD
The Peshitta · Syria
The Peshitta — the Bible in Syriac Aramaic and MILTHA's anchor translation. Its Old Testament was translated from Hebrew around the 1st–2nd century AD; its New Testament reached its standard form by the early 5th century, with 22 books (it lacks 2 Peter, 2–3 John, Jude and Revelation). In continuous use in the Syriac churches for over 1,500 years.
SOURCE: PESHITTA INSTITUTE, LEIDEN · STILL IN LITURGICAL USE TODAY
🪨
THE PILATE STONE
DISCOVERED: 1961 · CAESAREA MARITIMA
A limestone block bearing a Latin inscription: "Pontius Pilate, Prefect of Judea." Found during theater excavations. Now in the Israel Museum, Jerusalem.
John 19:1 — "Then Pilate took Jesus and flogged him." The governor named in all four Gospels is confirmed by a Roman inscription bearing his own name and title.
SOURCE: ISRAEL ANTIQUITIES AUTHORITY · EXCAVATED BY ANTONIO FROVA · 1961
🏛️
THE POOL OF SILOAM
EXCAVATED: 2004 · CITY OF DAVID, JERUSALEM
A large stepped pool from the Second Temple period, discovered during sewer repair work. Dated to the first century by pottery and coins.
John 9:7 — "Go, wash in the Pool of Siloam." The steps where the blind man walked down are still there. The channel through which the healing water flowed has been identified.
SOURCE: ISRAEL ANTIQUITIES AUTHORITY · RONNY REICH AND ELI SHUKRON · 2004
⚱️
THE OSSUARY OF CAIAPHAS
DISCOVERED: 1990 · JERUSALEM
A bone box inscribed "Yehosef bar Qayafa" — Joseph son of Caiaphas. Found in a burial cave in Jerusalem. It held the remains of several people, including a man of about sixty. Now in the Israel Museum.
John 18:13 — "They led him first to Annas, the father-in-law of Caiaphas." The high priest who presided over Jesus's trial has a verified burial. He was a real man making a real decision.
SOURCE: ISRAEL ANTIQUITIES AUTHORITY · ZIAS AND REICH · 1992
📜
JOHN RYLANDS PAPYRUS P52
DATED: c. 100–150 AD · EGYPT
One of the oldest surviving New Testament fragments, containing John 18:31-33 and 18:37-38. Written in Greek. Found in Egypt. Now at the John Rylands Library, Manchester.
Shows John's Gospel was being copied and read in Egypt, far from where it was written, within a few decades of its composition — strong evidence against a late origin.
SOURCE: JOHN RYLANDS LIBRARY · UNIVERSITY OF MANCHESTER · C.H. ROBERTS · 1934
📜
THE DEAD SEA SCROLLS
DISCOVERED: 1947 · QUMRAN, ISRAEL
Over 900 manuscripts including the complete Isaiah scroll. Dated by handwriting and carbon testing from about 250 BC to 68 AD. One thousand years older than any previously known Hebrew manuscripts.
The Great Isaiah Scroll agrees closely with the Masoretic text in modern Bibles, with mostly minor spelling and wording differences — strong evidence of careful preservation over a thousand years. Isaiah 53 was in the scroll more than a century before Christ.
SOURCE: ISRAEL ANTIQUITIES AUTHORITY · THE SHRINE OF THE BOOK, JERUSALEM
⛵
THE SEA OF GALILEE BOAT
DISCOVERED: 1986 · SEA OF GALILEE
A first-century wooden fishing boat recovered from the mud of the Sea of Galilee during a drought. Carbon dated to roughly 120 BC–40 AD. Now at the Yigal Allon Museum.
Mark 4:37-38 — "A great windstorm arose..." The kind of boat Jesus and the disciples used on the Sea of Galilee can now be seen in a museum on its shore.
SOURCE: YIGAL ALLON MUSEUM · KIBBUTZ GINOSAR · RADIOCARBON DATED
TACITUS
ANNALS, BOOK XV, SECTION 44 · c. 116 AD · ROMAN SENATOR AND HISTORIAN
"Christus, from whom the name had its origin, suffered the extreme penalty during the reign of Tiberius at the hands of one of our procurators, Pontius Pilatus, and a most mischievous superstition, thus checked for the moment, again broke out not only in Judaea, the first source of the evil, but even in Rome..."
A Roman senator writing with explicit hostility toward Christianity independently confirms the execution of Christ under Pontius Pilate during the reign of Tiberius. Tacitus had no motive to support Christian claims — he calls Christianity a "mischievous superstition." Yet his account agrees with the Gospels on who, where and under whom.
SOURCE: TACITUS · ANNALS · BOOK XV · SECTION 44 · LOEB CLASSICAL LIBRARY
FLAVIUS JOSEPHUS
ANTIQUITIES OF THE JEWS, BOOK XX · c. 93 AD · FIRST-CENTURY JEWISH HISTORIAN
"...the brother of Jesus who was called Christ, whose name was James, and some others...he brought them before them, and accused them, and delivered them to be stoned."
A first-century Jewish historian writing for a Roman audience with no motive to support Christian claims. His casual reference to "Jesus who was called Christ" demonstrates that Jesus's existence was uncontested historical fact in the first century — requiring no argument or defense.
SOURCE: JOSEPHUS · JEWISH ANTIQUITIES · BOOK XX · LOEB CLASSICAL LIBRARY
PLINY THE YOUNGER
LETTERS, BOOK X, LETTER 96 · c. 112 AD · ROMAN GOVERNOR OF BITHYNIA-PONTUS
"They were in the habit of meeting on a certain fixed day before it was light and singing an anthem to Christ as to a god, and binding themselves by a solemn oath, not to any wicked deeds, but never to commit any fraud, theft or adultery, never to falsify their word..."
A Roman governor writing to Emperor Trajan for legal guidance confirms that within 80 years of the crucifixion, communities in his province were worshipping Christ "as to a god." He describes practices matching exactly what the New Testament records.
SOURCE: PLINY THE YOUNGER · LETTERS · BOOK X · LETTER 96 · LOEB CLASSICAL LIBRARY
THALLUS
HISTORIES · DATE UNCERTAIN (PERHAPS MID-1ST CENTURY AD) · KNOWN ONLY THROUGH JULIUS AFRICANUS, c. 221 AD
According to Julius Africanus, Thallus explained the darkness at the crucifixion as a solar eclipse. If Thallus wrote about it, as Africanus says, this may be the earliest non-Christian reference to the event — but his work is lost and his date is uncertain.
Africanus answered that a solar eclipse cannot happen at Passover, which falls at full moon — so a natural eclipse could not explain the darkness. The account suggests the darkness was a known claim that non-believers felt they needed to explain.
SOURCE: JULIUS AFRICANUS · CHRONOGRAPHY · c. 221 AD · PRESERVED BY GEORGE SYNCELLUS
